01 — The problem

A single reading
says very little.

Microgravity, isolation, disrupted sleep, stress and months away from any hospital. The health data exists, but it arrives in pieces and is looked at separately.

Heart rate Heart rate variability Temperature Blood oxygen Sleep Activity Breathing Cabin CO₂ Recovery
One single reading

What matters is what happens when several change at once.
XPaze was built to read them together.

02 — The idea

It doesn’t ask how much.
It asks if it’s normal.

Other devices tell you “108 bpm”. XPaze asks a far more useful question: is it normal for this person to be at 108 bpm right now?

03 — The system

Three parts.
One reading.

It isn’t one more gadget to wear. It combines what is already on board with what the astronaut already carries.

A

The XPaze station

Fixed in the spacecraft or habitat. Tap your card, grip the two handles and, in a single gesture, it measures ECG, heart rate, oxygen and contactless temperature.

  • ECG
  • Heart rate
  • SpO₂
  • Temperature
  • Cabin air

B

Their watch, if they wear one

If they already wear a compatible watch, XPaze adds its heart rate, sleep and activity. If not, everything works the same. It’s one more source, not a requirement.

  • Sleep
  • Activity
  • Continuous heart rate

C

The app

On the station’s phone, connected over WiFi to the Raspberry Pi with no internet. It sums up the status at a glance, explains any change and also looks after mood and attention.

  • No internet
  • Explanations
  • Privacy

    04 — Your normal

    Every body
    is different.

    XPaze gradually builds your baseline: how your body normally behaves. So a reading isn’t only compared with the population average, but with you.

    An alert without an explanation is of little use.

    XPaze says what it saw and why it raised an alert, in plain sentences. It doesn’t label or diagnose: it detects the change, explains it and leaves the decisions to professionals.

    • Compares you with you, not with others
    • Rules out what exercise explains
    • Only alerts if the pattern persists

    05 — The mind

    The mind
    travels too.

    Months of isolation, confinement and disrupted sleep also weigh on mood and attention. XPaze applies the same idea: your own normal, sustained changes and a clear explanation. No diagnoses.

    Listens and answers by voice

    Meet Paze

    Your on-board psychologist.

    Paze talks with you by voice, knows your data (sleep, heart rate, check-ups, cabin air and space weather) and remembers what you talk about from one session to the next. It doesn’t diagnose: it keeps you company, suggests evidence-based techniques and, if something is serious, puts you in touch with a person.

    Talk to Paze
    Mood in 30 seconds

    Three sliders a day: how you feel, how much energy you have and how much tension you notice.

    Psychomotor vigilance

    A short reaction test. Lapses and slowness say a lot about fatigue.

    Sleep and fatigue

    The watch provides sleep hours and quality; you add the workload you perceive.

    Stress from HRV

    Heart rate variability and resting heart rate are compared with your own, not with anyone else’s.

    Crew cohesion

    A few short questions about living together and isolation, to notice when the mood on board changes.

    Private journal

    What you write never leaves the device. Nobody else reads it.

    Guided breathing

    Short breaks and exercises, suggested from your pattern over the last few days.

        07 — Inside

        This is how
        the station is built.

        The prototype, piece by piece. Keep scrolling to take it apart.

        0 / 8

        1. Station assembled

          200 × 150 × 70 mm, 3D-printed, with a removable tray. Eight steps to open it.

        2. Remove the phone

          It just rests on the tower, with no cables. It talks to the station over WiFi.

        3. Remove the caps

          They press-fit onto the top of the handles. The right one holds the heart rate and oxygen sensor.

        4. Pull out the handles

          Each is wrapped in copper tape, which acts as an ECG electrode. Their cables run down inside the tube.

        5. Undo the four screws

          M3 × 12 mm screws, one in each corner of the lid.

        6. Lift the lid

          It comes off with the phone tower. Inside are the temperature, distance and environment sensors.

        7. Slide out the tray

          All the electronics sit on a tray that comes out in one piece, fully connected.

        8. Separate the electronics

          Raspberry Pi, Arduino, ECG module, RFID reader and mini breadboard. Each in its own frame.

        9. Remove the battery

          A 10,000 mAh battery between two rails. It provides autonomy and isolates the ECG from mains power.

        The components.

        Accessible, modular hardware. No sensor is essential: each new technology joins as one more module.

        Raspberry Pi
        The local brain: history, baseline, anomalies and the app.
        Arduino Uno
        Reads the sensors at a steady rate and sends them over USB.
        AD8232 + copper
        ECG with the handles as electrodes.
        MAX30102
        Heart rate and oxygen at the thumb.
        MLX90614
        Contactless forehead temperature.
        VL53L0X
        Correct distance before measuring.
        AHT20 + BMP280
        Cabin temperature, humidity and pressure.
        RC522
        Identifies each astronaut by their card.

        How it thinks.

        Lightweight, fast and explainable models, chosen to run on little power and little data per person.

        1. Context

          Rest, exercise or sleep. The same heart rate means different things.

        2. Baseline

          Median and spread of your last few weeks, for each signal.

        3. Deviation

          Robust z-score (median and MAD): how far each signal strays from your normal.

        4. Drift

          EWMA to spot slow changes that hold day after day.

        5. Joint rarity

          Isolation Forest: rare combinations even when no single signal is extreme.

        6. Explanation

          Each contribution becomes a sentence anyone can understand.

        09 — Prototype

        What already works.
        And what comes next.

        It is not a certified medical device. It is proof that the idea works.

        Built

        • Station with an Arduino Uno and a Raspberry Pi. The Pi advertises itself over Bluetooth and WiFi: no cables to the phone.
        • App with a 5-step guided check-up: heart rate and oxygen (sensor or by hand), blood pressure, glucose, mood and sleep, and warning signs.
        • Traffic light with fixed, citable thresholds (NEWS2, ACC/AHA, ADA, PHQ-2, AASM and spaceflight habitability standards). The AI explains it; it never changes a color.
        • Paze: a psychologist by voice and text, with memory between sessions and your data as context.
        • Live space weather, assessed for the mission environment.
        • Real data from a Galaxy Watch via Samsung Health, with the XPaze Link Android app.

        Simulated, and what comes next

        • Simulated: the crew and the mission environment.
        • Next: a single Bluetooth chip (ESP32) for a smaller station.
        • Next: weeks-long trends with a dedicated model.
        • Next: ECG and contactless temperature built into the physical station.
